# Case-study summary

*A one-paragraph version to adapt for the portfolio page.*

---

**Play Studies** is a set of twelve browser toys, grouped into four families, that each turn a single action into a small workout for a specific cognitive skill. Holding trains impulse control, sustained attention, and time perception. Drawing trains cross-sensory mapping, working memory, and pattern prediction. Letting go trains systems thinking, mental simulation, and causal reasoning. Looking trains visual tracking, visual search, and mental rotation. I designed each toy from the skill outward, then spent the effort on how the mechanic feels: the acceleration of a bloom you cannot rush, the moment a doodle becomes music, a flung seed curving to a mark, a shape turning into its silhouette. The whole suite runs on one calm rounded typeface chosen from readability research, one visual language, and a quiet label that names the skill without breaking the play. It is a study in embodied inquiry, the idea that a skill lands harder when you do it than when you are taught it, and it doubles as a demonstration of how I move from a cognitive principle to a mechanic to a finished, felt interaction.

---

## The families, named

- **Hold → self-paced control.** Patience (impulse control), Steady (sustained attention), Count (time perception).
- **Draw → gesture into sense.** Melody (cross-sensory mapping), Recall (working memory), Continue (pattern prediction).
- **Let go → rules into outcomes.** Constellations (systems thinking), Aim (mental simulation), Tinker (causal reasoning).
- **Look → seeing closely.** Follow (visual tracking), Fog (visual search), Turn (mental rotation).
- **Across all twelve → embodied inquiry.** The interaction is the explanation.
